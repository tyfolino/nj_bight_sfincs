Hurricane Sandy compound-flood hindcast · weekly brief · 30 Sep – 8 Oct 2026 · updated Thu 8 Oct

Waves help the bigger model as much as they help v3. What still separates the two is mostly rain ponds and Raritan Bay.

The re-frozen v4 (run B) now has its waves-on score: waves cut its high-water-mark error from 0.57 to 0.50 m, the same gain v3 gets. v4 still misses the marks it shares with v3 by more, but three-quarters of that difference sits on five marks beside coastal lakes, where v3 lets no rain soak in. The real shortfall left is western Raritan Bay, ~0.4 m low. The final re-freeze runs with the corrected wind are waiting for nodes.

v3 — the finished model of the NJ ocean coast, Cape May → the Narrows. Our reference.
v4 — v3 plus Delaware Bay and river, the Raritan, Newark Bay; the far shores computed too.
HWM — high-water mark: a surveyed post-storm flood line. We report the typical miss (RMSE) and the average over/under (bias).
CSI — flood-extent agreement with FEMA's mapped Sandy flooding (0–1, higher is better).
NACCS — the Army Corps' North Atlantic Coast Comprehensive Study: surge + wave runs of Sandy that set our sea boundary.
SnapWave / waves-off twin — the wave solver inside SFINCS; the twin is the same run without it.
ERA5 — the global weather reanalysis that supplies our wind and air pressure (~25 km cells).
Head of tide — how far up a river the tide reaches today.

newMost of the v4–v3 gap is five rain ponds

75 % of v4's extra error sits on five marks beside coastal lakes (Wreck Pond and its neighbours). v3 has no infiltration, so rain keeps filling those lakes until the run ends; it matches the marks for the wrong reason. 13 % is one mark behind the Keansburg levee, missing from run B and restored in the re-freeze. Leave those six out and v4 scores 0.33 m against v3's 0.29 (a simple recount, not a paired test).

One rule for every river bed

Where no survey existed, the model filled tidal channels with land heights (grey line, up to +7 m — the Maurice was blocked). Now every channel takes the best source in a fixed order: Army Corps surveys → NOAA's CUDEM elevation model → NOAA charts → a sloping-river estimate (red). 32 tidal reaches, 48 km².

v3 modelled the Keansburg levee as a weir (a crest water must top); v4 silently dropped it, flooding the pocket behind to ~3.3 m where marks say ~1.6 m (v3, with the weir: 2.6 m). Restored, with the Overpeck Creek tidal dam (crest 1.74 m) added the same way.

Why Raritan Bay is low: v3 borrowed the answer

Peak water level from the Narrows (left) into Raritan Bay. v3 (orange) was told NACCS's level at the Arthur Kill mouth, the bay's downwind end. v4 (blue) has to build the rise itself and makes about 0.18 m of the observed ~0.67 m (black diamonds; green = NACCS). Waves off.

11

Is it the engine? No — but the wind was misplaced

Left: in a toy 20 × 5 km basin under steady wind, our SFINCS matches the textbook setup within 1–2 % in all 36 runs. Right: a bug we found on the way — the engine read every gridded wind and pressure value half an ERA5 cell to the north-west, giving the NY bays only 0.73–0.86 of the intended wind push. Fixed and proven today; every run so far carries it.

12

The weather models are too weak over the bays

At Robbins Reef light (NY Harbor), every gridded product reads 0.59–0.76 of the observed wind over the storm peak; they treat the narrow bays as land. NACCS's own winds read 0.99. Offshore, at the buoys, all products do fine (mostly within 10 %). Maps: mean wind 29 Oct 21:00 → 30 Oct 03:00 UTC.
